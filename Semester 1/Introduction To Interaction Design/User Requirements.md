Who are users
- very different 
- Mental and physical

Human Cognition
- Core aspects
	- Attention
		- What to concentrate on
		- What part of the product do we want to direct attention to
		- Leave out clutter
	- Perception
		- How information is acquired and translated into experiences
		- Sight and sound are main senses used
		- Colour of text and background affect perception of information
	- Memory
		- Recognition vs Recall
			- "Hello, my friend"
			- What is his name?
			- Recognition is easier than recall
		- Keep procedures short and simple
	- Reading, speaking, listening
		- Reading 
			- quicker
			- more skilful
			- easy to skip
		- Listening
			- Temporary
			- Needs recall
			- Max of 3 pieces of info in short term memory
	- Problem solving
		- Reflective cognition

Mental Models
- Formed during interaction
	- Represent a users understanding of a system
	- What does it do
	- How can I use it

Personas
- Roleplay as an idiot
- Capture important user characteristics of user base
	- realistic
- Not just necessary things, bring them to life
- Be very careful about biases when making personas
	- sexist ageist ablest etc

User Requirements
- Functional
	- Services provided
	- What not How
	- "The robot must be able to climb stairs"
- Non-functional
	- Constraints e.g. run on certain platforms
	- Appearance, usability
	- "The robot must avoid obstacles within 3 seconds"
- Categorising requirements
	- MoSCow - Must, Should, Could, Won't

Why work with users
- Focus only on development
	- Poor communication
	- Believing developers way is best
	- Inevitably have more experience with product than user
- Outcome
	- Non "user-friendly" systems
	- Users need lots of training to use system
	- System may not be accepted by user community
