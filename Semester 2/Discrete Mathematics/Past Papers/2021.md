Let $P(n)$ be the statement $5 | 6^n - 1$

$P(1)$ states:
$5 | 6^1 - 1$
$5 | 6-1$
$5|5$
This is true, so $P(1)$ holds

$P(k)$ states $5 | 6^k - 1$
Assume $P(k)$ holds
Therefore there must be an integer $m$ such that:
$5m = 6^k -1$
$5m + 1 = 6^k$
We must show that $P(k+1) holds:
$5 | 6^{k+1} - 1$
$6^{k+1} - 1$
$= 6 * 6^k - 1$
$= 6(5m+1) - 1$
$= 30m  + 6 - 1$
$= 30m + 5$
$= 5(6m+1)$
Since $6m+1$ is an integer, this expression is divisible by 5.
So, $P(k+1)$ holds
Thus we see $P(k+1)$ follows from $P(k)$

By the principle of mathematical induction, $P(n)$ is true for any positive integer $n$
